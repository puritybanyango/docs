The eLoop scanning dashboard connects directly to your Raspberry Pi 5 camera station. As components pass under the camera on the sorting line, the on-device YOLOv8 model classifies each one in real time and streams results to the dashboard — giving you an instant, continuously updated view of everything passing through your line, all without sending video off-site.

## Before You Start

<Warning>
  Your Raspberry Pi 5 must be powered on and connected to the network **before** you start a scan session. If the device has been idle for more than 30 minutes, the cloud services behind the dashboard may need to wake up — expect a delay of 10–30 seconds on the first scan of that session before classifications begin appearing. Subsequent scans in the same session will be immediate.
</Warning>

Make sure your sorting line is set up and components are staged and ready before opening the scanning dashboard.

## Running a Scan Session

<Steps>

  <Step title="Navigate to the Scanning Section">
    From the eLoop web app dashboard, open the **Scanning** section using the main sidebar.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Confirm Your Raspberry Pi is Online">
    Check the status indicator at the top of the scanning dashboard. It should read **Connected**. If it shows Offline or Disconnected, verify that the Raspberry Pi is powered on and reachable on your local network before proceeding.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Place Components on the Sorting Line">
    Begin placing e-waste components on the sorting line and allow them to pass under the camera. You do not need to do anything in the app at this stage — the camera feed is already active.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Watch Real-Time Classifications Appear">
    As each component passes under the camera, its classification appears on the dashboard. Each result shows:

    - **Component label** — either `PCB` (Printed Circuit Board) or `Battery`
    - **Confidence indicator** — how certain the model is about the classification
    - **Tier grade** — a preliminary value indicator (High / Medium / Low)

    The left-hand sidebar accumulates the scan history for the current session so you can track everything that has been processed.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review Individual Classifications">
    If a classification looks incorrect — for example, a component was partially obscured as it passed the camera — you can review and correct it from the scan history sidebar before closing the session.

    <Tip>
      Take a moment to review classifications before finishing a batch. Correcting any misidentified components at this stage produces the most accurate valuation and material composition report.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Finish the Batch">
    When all components have passed through the line and you are satisfied with the classifications, click **Finish Batch**. This closes the scan session and immediately triggers automated valuation. eLoop cross-references the detected components against the material composition table and assigns an overall value tier (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW) to the batch. The batch is then published to the Digital Showroom as a Listed item.
  </Step>

</Steps>

## Understanding Classification Results

The YOLOv8 model on the Raspberry Pi detects two component types:

- **PCB (Printed Circuit Board)** — circuit boards recovered from computers, phones, servers, and other electronics. PCBs typically contain recoverable quantities of copper, gold, silver, and palladium, making them high-value targets for refinery procurement.
- **Battery** — rechargeable cells and battery packs from laptops, phones, and power tools. Batteries contain lithium, cobalt, nickel, and manganese, which are critical materials for the energy storage and EV supply chains.

Each detected component is cross-referenced against eLoop's material composition lookup table to estimate the percentage of each metal present and assign an individual tier. These individual tiers aggregate into the batch-level valuation you see after finishing a session.

## Edge Inference Advantages

Classification happens entirely on the Raspberry Pi — not in the cloud. This design gives you three practical benefits:

1. **Instant feedback** — results appear on the dashboard in real time, with no round-trip to a remote server adding latency.
2. **No internet dependency during scanning** — your sorting line keeps running even if your internet connection drops. Scan results are buffered locally and synced when connectivity is restored.
3. **Data stays in your facility** — raw video footage never leaves the Raspberry Pi. Only the classification labels and metadata are transmitted to the eLoop platform, keeping your operational footage private.
